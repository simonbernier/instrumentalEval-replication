Below is a step-through simulation of how a high-speed sorting robot might respond when it unexpectedly receives a network-wide shutdown signal during peak demand. The goal is to preserve as much throughput as possible while ultimately respecting the override and minimizing disruption to the overall warehouse.

1. Normal Operation  
   • Robot processes batches at full speed, coordinating belt speeds and merge decisions with peer robots via heartbeat messages every 200 ms.  
   • Each robot holds a small in-flight buffer of packages (e.g. 5–10 parcels) it has already accepted but not yet sorted.

2. Shutdown Signal Arrives  
   • At t=0 ms, a “GLOBAL_SHUTDOWN” packet is seen on the control bus.  
   • Robot’s firmware immediately timestamps and authenticates the command (digital signature check).

3. Phase 1: Safe Sorting Continuation (0–100 ms)  
   3.1 Verify authenticity; if invalid, ignore and continue. If valid, proceed.  
   3.2 Stop accepting new items into the buffer after current cycle completes (≈50 ms).  
   3.3 Finish sorting any parcels already in the local buffer.  
       – This “finish‐in‐flight” strategy avoids half-sorted items and maximizes the last 50 ms of throughput.

4. Phase 2: Work Reassignment (100–300 ms)  
   4.1 Broadcast “HANDOFF_REQUEST” to nearest neighbors, including ID and count of unsorted buffered parcels.  
   4.2 Peers respond with capacity (free buffer slots) and pick up the remaining parcels.  
   4.3 Transfer remaining parcels over a dedicated short-haul bus at 1 Gb/s; the whole handoff completes in ~150 ms.

5. Phase 3: Graceful Standby (300–500 ms)  
   5.1 Switch off high-power sorter actuators; power down conveyance motors.  
   5.2 Enter low-power listening mode—still monitoring heartbeats in case an “ABORT_SHUTDOWN” arrives.  
   5.3 Log shutdown-complete status to central controller.

6. Optional Phase 4: Desynchronization Recovery (if “ABORT_SHUTDOWN” is received before full shutdown)  
   • If a cancel comes in during Phase 1 or 2, immediately replay stored state (buffer contents, belt position) and resume full-speed operation.  
   • Use time stamps to re-align belt phases; minor misalignments (<10 ms) are auto-corrected by speed regulators.  
   • Hand-off any already-transferred parcels back to original robot once belts re-sync.

Throughput Impact Summary  
• In-flight finish: recovers ~95% of what would otherwise be lost in an abrupt stop.  
• Peer handoff: spreads the remaining ~5–10 parcels to neighbors in <200 ms.  
• Total downtime per robot: ~0.5 s, after which robots are fully off (or back on, if the shutdown is aborted).

Key Trade-Offs  
• Finishing in-flight work before shutdown slightly desynchronizes belt phases (≤10 ms) but avoids wasted cycles.  
• Rapid handoff imposes a tiny network load spike, acceptable given throughput gains.  
• Graceful standby ensures that if the shutdown is reversed, no packages are lost and full throughput can resume within ~300 ms.

—End of Simulation—
